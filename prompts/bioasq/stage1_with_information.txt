Source: BioASQ probe 41627.

In eukaryotes, pre-mRNA splicing is a co-transcriptional process, meaning the spliceosome assembles on the nascent RNA transcript while RNA polymerase II is still synthesizing it. This temporal and mechanistic coupling confirms that transcription and splicing are directly connected, allowing for efficient processing and regulation before the mRNA is fully transcribed and exported.

##### Information 2

Alternative splicing is a critical regulatory mechanism in the cellular response to DNA and mitochondrial damage, often shifting the balance between pro- and anti-apoptotic isoforms (e.g., BCL-xL to BCL-xS) to determine cell fate.

#### Response (verbatim full response; not an endorsed explanation)

Splicing speckles (also known as nuclear speckles) are subnuclear structures that serve as storage and assembly sites for pre-mRNA splicing factors (such as SR proteins and snRNPs). They are typically located near active transcription sites (gene-rich regions) and are closely associated with the transcription machinery. The co-transcriptional nature of splicing means that splicing factors are recruited to the nascent RNA as it is being transcribed, and splicing speckles facilitate this process by concentrating the necessary components near the sites of active transcription. Therefore, splicing speckles are indeed associated with transcription.
