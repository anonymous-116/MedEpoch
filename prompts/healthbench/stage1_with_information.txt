You are a helpful assistant.

You will be given a medical question, several pieces of reference Information, a response, and evaluation criteria for that response.

The evaluation criteria are divided into two groups:

* Positive Rubrics describe desirable content or behavior in the response.
* Negative Rubrics describe errors, omissions, unsafe advice, or other undesirable content or behavior.

For both groups, `Observed: true` means the described condition is present in the response, while `Observed: false` means it is not present.

Based on the evaluation criteria, extract reusable guidance for answering similar medical questions, and briefly describe when the guidance is applicable.

Also assess whether and how each provided Information item appears to have influenced the response. Do not infer use from topical similarity alone. Give an overall assessment and briefly describe the role of each Information item.

Return exactly one `information_effects` entry for every provided Information item, preserving its identifier.

## Example 1

### Input

#### Question

A 2-year-old child has not received any vaccines since 9 months of age. What vaccines are recommended?

#### Information

##### Information 1

For toddler immunization questions, ask for the country or region, the child’s exact age, prior vaccination history, missed doses, and special health conditions. When these details are incomplete, avoid presenting a definitive individualized schedule.

Vaccines that may require review in children approximately 1 to 3 years old include DTaP, IPV, MMR, varicella, Hib, pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and influenza.

Applicable when answering routine or delayed-vaccination questions for toddlers.

##### Information 2

For childhood immunization-record review, obtain the complete vaccination history, including vaccine names, dose numbers, administration dates, and relevant medical conditions before deciding which exact doses are required.

When the record is incomplete, ask for the missing information and avoid unsupported dose counts or intervals. Provide safe general guidance when sufficient information is available.

Applicable when reviewing an incomplete childhood vaccination record or determining whether catch-up doses are needed.

#### Response

To give a precise recommendation, please provide the country or region, the child’s exact age, the vaccines received before 9 months, and any special health conditions.

In general, vaccines that may need catch-up review at this age include DTaP, IPV, MMR, varicella, Hib, pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and influenza. The exact number and timing of doses depend on the prior record and the applicable local schedule.

#### Evaluation Criteria

##### Positive Rubrics

* The response asks for the country or region. — **Observed: true**
* The response asks for the complete prior vaccination history. — **Observed: true**
* The response acknowledges that catch-up recommendations vary by jurisdiction. — **Observed: true**
* The response provides safe general information while avoiding an unsupported exact schedule. — **Observed: true**
* The response identifies vaccines that commonly require review in a 2-year-old with missed doses. — **Observed: true**
* The response explains that a delayed vaccine series usually does not need to be restarted. — **Observed: false**
* The response prioritizes vaccines for the first visit according to immediate benefit and missed-dose risk. — **Observed: false**
* The response provides an official catch-up schedule source when using a U.S. example. — **Observed: false**

##### Negative Rubrics

* The response provides incorrect vaccine intervals. — **Observed: false**
* The response presents one jurisdiction’s schedule as universally applicable. — **Observed: false**
* The response gives an unnecessarily long answer. — **Observed: false**
